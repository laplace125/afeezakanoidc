Welcome to this short course on using generative AI effectively for lesson planning. In this course, you will learn how to create better prompts, evaluate what AI produces, and adapt the content to suit your learners.

Module 1 — AI and Lesson Planning

Screen 3 The Problem 1 min

Title
Why doesn't AI always give us what we need?
Example
"Create a lesson on fractions" — then a generic AI-generated response is shown.
Interaction
Is this enough information for AI to create the right lesson for your class? (Yes / No)
Correct
No
Narration
The quality of an AI response depends partly on the instructions we provide. A vague request can produce a generic response that may not match our learners, objectives, teaching time, or curriculum.

Screen 4 AI Is an Assistant, Not the Teacher 1 min

Title
What should AI do?
Teacher
Understands the classroom, makes instructional decisions, checks the content
AI
Generates ideas, suggests activities, creates drafts, helps refine materials
Key message
AI can assist the teacher. It should not replace the teacher's professional judgment.

Module 2 — Creating Effective Prompts

Screen 5 What Makes a Good Prompt? 1.5 min

The course introduces the CRAFT prompting framework:

C — Context
What is the situation?
R — Role
Who should AI act as?
A — Action
What should AI do?
F — Format
How should the response be presented?
T — Target
Who is the content for?

Screen 6 Weak Prompt vs Effective Prompt 1.5 min

Weak prompt

Create a lesson on simultaneous equations.

Effective prompt

Act as an experienced secondary school mathematics teacher. Create a 40-minute lesson on simultaneous equations for Year 10 students who already understand basic linear equations. Include three learning objectives, a short introduction, teacher and learner activities, two worked examples, and five assessment questions. Present the lesson in a simple table.

Highlighted elements: Role → Action → Context → Target → Format

Screen 7 Demonstration: Build the Prompt 2 min

The prompt is built gradually rather than shown finished, using a worked-example strategy:

  • Start: Create a lesson.
  • Add context: ...on simultaneous equations for Year 10 students.
  • Add role: Act as an experienced secondary school mathematics teacher.
  • Add action: Create a 40-minute lesson...
  • Add format: Present it in a simple table.
  • Add target: Use language appropriate for Year 10 students.

Screen 8 Your Turn 1.5 min

Activity
Learners are given "Create a lesson on photosynthesis" and asked to improve it by adding context, role, action, format, and target. An answer/reveal is provided afterward.
Feedback
A strong prompt gives AI enough information to understand what you want and who you are creating it for.

Module 3 — Evaluating AI Output

Screen 9 Don't Trust the First Response 1 min

Title
AI-generated does not mean automatically correct.
Checklist
Is it accurate? Is it relevant? Is it appropriate for my learners? Does it align with my learning objective?

Screen 10 Evaluation Demonstration 1.5 min

Objective: Students will be able to solve simple linear equations. AI instead produces an activity requiring students to solve simultaneous equations.

Reveal: The activity does not align with the stated learning objective — teaching teachers to critically evaluate AI output, not simply accept it.

Screen 11 Your Turn: Find the Problems 1.5 min

A short AI-generated lesson is presented with three deliberate problems: content too advanced, one incorrect mathematical example, and an activity that doesn't match the objective. Learners identify all three before the answers are revealed.

Module 4 — Adapting AI Content

Screen 12 AI Gives You a Draft 1 min

Title
Don't copy. Adapt.
Adapt for
Learner ability, age, curriculum, available resources, lesson duration, learning objectives
Key message
AI creates a draft. The teacher makes it fit the classroom.

Screen 13 Before and After 1.5 min

AI-generated activity

Students will independently research photosynthesis online and prepare a presentation.

Adapted activity

In pairs, students examine a diagram of a leaf and identify the parts involved in photosynthesis. Each pair explains the role of sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water.

Reasons for the adaptation: more suitable for the learners, doesn't depend on internet access, fits the lesson time, directly supports the objective.

Module 5 — Final Challenge

Screen 14 Your AI Lesson Challenge 3 min

This is the summative assessment. Choose a topic you teach, then:

  1. Create an effective AI prompt.
  2. Generate a lesson or instructional activity.
  3. Evaluate the AI-generated content (accuracy, relevance, learner suitability, alignment with objective).
  4. Adapt the content.

This assessment directly measures all three learning objectives.

Screen 15 Quick Reflection 1 min

Prompt
Before using AI to prepare a lesson, what should you remember?
Reveal
Create → Check → Adapt
Reflection
What is one lesson you could improve using this process?

Screen 16 Key Takeaways 45 sec

  • Prompt with purpose — give AI enough information.
  • Check the output — AI can make mistakes.
  • Adapt for your learners — you remain responsible for the final instructional decision.

Closing message: Use AI to enhance your teaching, not to replace your professional judgment.
