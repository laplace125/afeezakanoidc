Artifact 4 — Development

Learning Materials

The instructional content, media, and worked examples developed to teach the CRAFT prompting framework and AI-evaluation skills.

Media strategy

The course combines five kinds of media, each chosen for a specific instructional purpose:

  • Microsoft PowerPoint / Canva — course slides and visual design, presenting learning materials in a clear, attractive, and engaging way.
  • Screen recording — demonstrating an AI interaction, showing the steps involved in entering instructions, generating responses, and reviewing the results.
  • Text — key concepts, definitions, and step-by-step instructions, presented simply so learners can follow the lesson.
  • Icons / diagrams — simplifying complex concepts and illustrating relationships between ideas.
  • Short quiz / activity — helping learners practise the skills they've learned and test their understanding.

The CRAFT prompting framework

The core teaching material of Module 2 is a five-part framework for writing effective AI prompts:

LetterElementGuiding question
CContextWhat is the situation?
RRoleWho should AI act as?
AActionWhat should AI do?
FFormatHow should the response be presented?
TTargetWho is the content for?

Worked example: building a prompt

Rather than presenting a finished prompt, the material builds it step by step so learners can see each CRAFT element being added:

Start

Create a lesson.

+ Context

Create a lesson on simultaneous equations for Year 10 students.

+ Role

Act as an experienced secondary school mathematics teacher.

+ Action

Create a 40-minute lesson…

+ Format

Present it in a simple table.

+ Target

Use language appropriate for Year 10 students.

Evaluation checklist

Module 3's core material is a four-question checklist teachers apply to any AI-generated content before using it:

  • Is it accurate?
  • Is it relevant?
  • Is it appropriate for my learners?
  • Does it align with my learning objective?

Worked example used in the material: the objective is "Students will be able to solve simple linear equations," but the AI produces an activity requiring simultaneous equations — a clear misalignment that learners are asked to spot.

Adaptation example: before and after

AI-generated activity

Students will independently research photosynthesis online and prepare a presentation.

Adapted activity

In pairs, students examine a diagram of a leaf and identify the parts involved in photosynthesis. Each pair explains the role of sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water.

The material explains why this adaptation works better: it's more suitable for the learners, doesn't depend on internet access, fits the lesson time, and directly supports the objective.

Closing summary material

The final screen distills the whole course into one memorable sequence, used as the last on-screen visual:

Create an effective prompt → Check what AI produces → Adapt it for your learners.
