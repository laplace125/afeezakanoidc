Artifact 5 — Development & Evaluation

Assessment

Formative checks and a practical, summative final assessment designed to measure all three learning objectives.

Assessment strategy

The course uses formative assessment during instruction and a practical final assessment at the end, so that learning is checked along the way rather than only at completion.

Formative assessment

After the prompting section, teachers answer a multiple-choice question in this style:

Which of the following prompts provides enough information for an AI tool to generate a useful mathematics lesson for students in Basic 5?

A second formative check follows the evaluation module, where teachers are shown a short AI-generated lesson containing three deliberate problems (content too advanced, one incorrect mathematical example, and an activity that doesn't match the objective) and asked to identify all three before the answers are revealed.

Final practical assessment

This is the summative task, and it is designed to directly measure all three learning objectives at once:

  1. Choose a topic and class you teach.
  2. Create a prompt that asks an AI tool to generate a lesson for your learners.
  3. Review the AI-generated lesson and identify at least three things you would check or improve.
  4. Adapt the lesson so it better meets the needs of your learners.

How the assessment maps to the objectives

ObjectiveAssessed by
Create an effective AI prompt using context, role, action, format, and target Step 2 — writing the prompt
Evaluate AI-generated content for accuracy, relevance, learner suitability, and alignment Step 3 — identifying at least three issues
Adapt AI-generated content for a specific group of learners Step 4 — submitting an improved version

Because the final task requires teachers to actually produce a prompt, critique real output, and revise it, the assessment is performance-based rather than a knowledge check — it measures whether teachers can do the skill, not just recognise it.
